Before you install

Your own Schwab app
You need your own free developer app at developer.schwab.com — an Individual (“Myself”) app. Approval takes a few days, so start this first. You will get a client ID and client secret.
An invitation
The app does nothing on its own. Andre has to grant you access before any signal can reach you — see step 4 below.
Nothing else
No Python, no accounts to create, no command line. One file, double-click.

Installing

Your computer will warn you. This is expected.

The app isn’t code-signed — that costs a few hundred dollars a year and this is a family project. Windows and macOS flag every unsigned app the same way, whether it’s harmless or not. Here’s how to get past it.

  1. Download the file

    Use the button above for your computer. It saves to your Downloads folder.

  2. Open it, and get past the warning

    On Windows: double-click it. If a blue box says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway.

    On Mac: don’t double-click it. Right-click (or Control-click) the file, choose Open, then click Open again in the box that appears. You only do this the first time.

    If macOS says the file is “damaged”, open Terminal and run:
    xattr -d com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/copier-agent

  3. A setup page opens in your browser

    That’s the app itself. It runs only on your computer — the address starts with 127.0.0.1, which means nothing is on the internet and no one else can reach it.

  4. Work through the six setup screens

    • Your name and contact details.
    • It creates your personal key and shows you a public key — send that to Andre. It’s safe to text. Your private half never leaves your computer.
    • It shows a fingerprint — four blocks of letters and numbers. Call Andre and read it out loud to confirm it matches. Don’t skip this and don’t do it over text; it’s the one check that proves signals really came from him.
    • Connect your Schwab account with your client ID and secret.
    • Set your risk limits — how much per trade, how many at once, daily stop.
    • Choose approve each trade or place automatically. Start with approve.

  5. Watch it for a week before arming it

    Practice mode runs everything normally but never sends an order to Schwab. You’ll see exactly what it would have done. Turn it off when you’re satisfied — not before.

What you should know

You’re in control
Your risk limits, your approve-or-automatic choice, your account. You can close the app at any time and everything stops. Nobody else can place a trade for you.
Schwab logs you out weekly
Schwab expires the connection every 7 days and there’s no way around it. The app shows a countdown and turns red when it’s close. When it asks, click Reconnect. This is the part people forget.
Small accounts
There is no day trade limit and no large minimum balance — the old pattern day trader rule was scrapped in June 2026. A margin account has to keep $2,000 in it. Past that, your broker decides your intraday buying power in real time from whatever you already hold, and going over that is what can get an account restricted. The app cannot see that number, so on a small account keep the number of open trades low.
You can lose money
This places real options and stock trades. Options can go to zero. Nothing here is advice, nothing is guaranteed, and you are responsible for your own account. Only use money you can afford to lose.
Your credentials stay put
Your Schwab login and keys are stored on your own machine in your operating system’s secure storage. They are never uploaded and Andre never sees them.

Trouble?

Check the setup guide first — its last section lists every warning the program can show you and what each one wants you to do.

Still stuck? Text Andre. Useful things to include: which screen you’re stuck on, what the message says word for word, and whether you’re on Windows or Mac. Screenshot the copier, never your Schwab app key page.
