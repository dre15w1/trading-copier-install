1. Nobody but you can trade your account

You create your own broker credentials and type them into the program on your own computer, where they stay. Andre never receives them, never holds trading authority over your account, and cannot place, change or cancel an order in it. Every order is placed by software running on your machine, under settings you choose. Nobody can see your balances, positions or results except you and your broker.

2. This is not advice, and it is not tailored to you

Andre is a software developer, not a financial professional. He is not a registered investment adviser or broker-dealer and holds no securities licence. Every signal is a description of a trade he is taking in his own account, sent in identical form to everybody. Nobody knows or considers your income, your tax situation, your other holdings, how much risk suits you, or anything else about you. No signal is an opinion that a trade is right for you. Deciding to install this, to connect it to an account, to choose any setting, and to act on any signal is your own decision.

3. You can lose everything in the account

The software is experimental and is changed often, sometimes several times a day. It has had bugs and it will have more. It runs on your own computer — if that computer is off, asleep or offline, the program cannot act at all, including to close a position. Signals can arrive late or not at all. Your fills will differ from Andre's, and can be a loss where his was a profit. It trades options, including same-day-expiry options that can lose their entire value within minutes. Stop-losses do not guarantee a price.

Losing the entire balance of the account is a realistic outcome, not a remote one. Do not connect this to money you cannot afford to lose completely.

4. Nobody is paid, and nobody is responsible

Andre receives no fee, commission or share of anything. In exchange, you accept the risk of what happens and release him and Walkers Software from claims for trading losses, including losses caused by ordinary mistakes in the software. That release does not cover gross negligence, deliberate wrongdoing or fraud, and it cannot waive any right you have under securities law.

5. What you are agreeing to keep private

The program, how it works, and the signals themselves are Andre's. You may not forward, republish or resell signals, or give the software to anyone else. Your own account data and trade history are yours, and you may share them freely with anyone, including a lawyer, an accountant or a regulator.

Before you install

Your own Schwab app
You need your own free developer app at developer.schwab.com — an Individual (“Myself”) app. Approval takes a few days, so start this first. You will get a client ID and client secret.
An invitation
The app does nothing on its own. Andre has to grant you access before any signal can reach you — see step 4 below.
Nothing else
No Python, no accounts to create, no command line. One file, double-click.

Installing

Your computer will warn you. This is expected.

The app isn’t code-signed — that costs a few hundred dollars a year and this is a family project. Windows and macOS flag every unsigned app the same way, whether it’s harmless or not. Here’s how to get past it.

  1. Download the file

    Use the button above for your computer. It saves to your Downloads folder.

  2. Open it, and get past the warning

    On Windows

    Double-click the file. If a blue box says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway. That’s it — you only do this the first time.

    On Mac

    Don’t double-click it. A Mac doesn’t recognise this kind of file as a program, so double-clicking opens it in TextEdit and fills the screen with gibberish. Nothing is broken when that happens — the Mac just needs to be told, once, that this is an app.

    It takes four short steps and about a minute. You never have to do it again.

    1. Open Terminal. Press ⌘ + Space, type Terminal, press Return. A window with a text prompt opens.
    2. Copy the three lines below using the Copy button, then click into the Terminal window and press ⌘ + V to paste them. 
      if [ "$(uname -m)" = "arm64" ]; then
  FILE="$HOME/Downloads/copier-agent-macos-apple-silicon"
else
  FILE="$HOME/Downloads/copier-agent-macos-intel"
fi
chmod +x "$FILE"
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine "$FILE"
"$FILE"
    3. Press Return. The copier starts and your browser opens by itself.
    4. Leave the Terminal window open. Closing it stops the copier and no trades will be copied. Minimise it instead.

    What those lines do, in order: the first tells your Mac the file is a program, the second clears the “downloaded from the internet” flag that makes macOS refuse to run it, and the third starts it.

    If something doesn’t match

    “No such file or directory” — the file isn’t in Downloads. Type chmod +x (with the space), then drag the file from Finder into the Terminal window. It fills in the real location for you. Do the same for the other two lines.

    “No such xattr” — nothing to clear. That’s fine, carry on.

    The file is called a Mac filename ending in (1) — you downloaded it twice. Delete both, download once more, and start again. A name with (1) in it won’t match the lines above.

    Someone texted or AirDropped you the file — download it yourself from the button at the top of this page instead. Sending a program through Messages strips the very setting step 2 puts back, and it will keep failing.

  3. A setup page opens in your browser

    That’s the app itself. It runs only on your computer — the address starts with 127.0.0.1, which means nothing is on the internet and no one else can reach it.

  4. Work through the six setup screens

    • Your name and contact details.
    • It creates your personal key and shows a TV1- device code. Click Copy device code and send that code to Andre. Your private key never leaves your computer.
    • It shows a fingerprint — four blocks of letters and numbers. Call Andre and read it out loud to confirm it matches. Don’t skip this and don’t do it over text; it’s the one check that proves signals really came from him.
    • Connect your Schwab account with your client ID and secret.
    • Set your risk limits — how much per trade, how many at once, daily stop.
    • Choose approve each trade or place automatically. Start with approve.

  5. Watch it for a week before arming it

    Practice mode runs everything normally but never sends an order to Schwab. You’ll see exactly what it would have done. Turn it off when you’re satisfied — not before.

What you should know

You’re in control
Your risk limits, your approve-or-automatic choice, your account. You can close the app at any time and everything stops. Nobody else can place a trade for you.
Schwab logs you out weekly
Schwab expires the connection every 7 days and there’s no way around it. The app shows a countdown and turns red when it’s close. When it asks, click Reconnect. This is the part people forget.
Small accounts
There is no day trade limit and no large minimum balance — the old pattern day trader rule was scrapped in June 2026. A margin account has to keep $2,000 in it. Past that, your broker decides your intraday buying power in real time from whatever you already hold, and going over that is what can get an account restricted. The app cannot see that number, so on a small account keep the number of open trades low.
You can lose money
This places real options and stock trades. Options can go to zero. Nothing here is advice, nothing is guaranteed, and you are responsible for your own account. Only use money you can afford to lose.
Your credentials stay put
Your Schwab login and keys are stored on your own machine in your operating system’s secure storage. They are never uploaded and Andre never sees them.
